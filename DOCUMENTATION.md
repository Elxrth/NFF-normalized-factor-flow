# NFF — Normalized Factor Flow

<p align="center">
  <strong>Structure inside the flow.</strong>
</p>

<p align="center">
  A framework for transforming continuous data flows into normalized
  structural representations through temporal Containers, NUA analysis,
  normalization, signatures, and ORDER.
</p>

<br>

---

## 1. Core Concept

NFF is based on the idea that a continuous data flow can contain detectable structure even when its individual values appear unpredictable.

Rather than attempting to predict every individual value, NFF analyzes the statistical, temporal, spectral, and potentially nonlinear properties of the flow as a whole.

NFF can therefore be applied to different types of flows, including:

* **Periodic** — repeating structures such as sine waves.
* **Stochastic** — flows dominated by probabilistic behavior.
* **Chaotic** — deterministic systems exhibiting complex observable behavior.
* **Continuous** — real-time measurements generated by arbitrary sources.

---

## 2. Flow

A **Flow** is a continuous or discrete sequence of values generated by a source.

$$
F = {x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n}
$$

Possible sources include:

* Mathematical functions
* Sensors
* Weather measurements
* Financial data
* Biological signals
* EEG recordings
* Industrial measurements

The Flow represents the raw temporal information on which NFF operates.

---

## 3. Containers

NFF divides a Flow into temporal **Containers**.

A Container contains every value generated during a predefined period called the **Container Delay**.

$$
C_i = {x(t) \mid iD \leq t < (i+1)D}
$$

Where:

* $C_i$ is the $i$-th Container.
* $D$ is the Container Delay.
* $x(t)$ represents the Flow at time $t$.

This segmentation preserves the temporal evolution of the Flow instead of treating the entire dataset as one static object.

---

## 4. NUA — Normalized Units Amount

A **NUA (Normalized Units Amount)** is an independent analysis unit responsible for measuring a specific property of one or more Containers.

An NFF system may contain any number of NUA units.

Examples of measurable properties include:

* Mean
* Variance
* Temporal variation
* Autocorrelation
* Entropy
* Spectral entropy
* Dominant frequency
* Complexity
* Predictability
* Other statistical or structural characteristics

The NUA collection can be represented as:

$$
U = {u_1, u_2, \ldots, u_n}
$$

The purpose of multiple NUA units is to observe the same Flow from several mathematical perspectives.

---

## 5. Normalization

Different measurements can have completely different numerical scales.

NFF normalizes their outputs before combining them.

A standard min-max normalization can be expressed as:

$$
u'*i =
\frac{u_i-u*{\min}}
{u_{\max}-u_{\min}}
$$

The normalized measurements form the basis of the **NFF Signature**.

The normalization method itself is part of the NFF configuration and therefore affects the resulting representation.

---

## 6. NFF Signature

The **NFF Signature** is the multidimensional representation generated by the NUA layer.

$$
S = {s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_n}
$$

Each value represents a normalized characteristic detected within the Flow.

The Signature preserves the individual dimensions measured by the NUA units.

This distinguishes it from a single scalar value such as ORDER.

---

## 7. ORDER

**ORDER** is a scalar representation obtained by aggregating the normalized NUA measurements.

$$
ORDER = \sum_i w_i s_i
$$

The weights may satisfy:

$$
\sum_i w_i = 1
$$

The weighting strategy determines the relative contribution of each NUA to the final ORDER value.

> **Important:** ORDER should not automatically be interpreted as an absolute measurement of mathematical order.

ORDER is a value produced by a specific NFF configuration and represents the structural characteristics detected by that configuration.

Consequently, two NFF systems using different NUA sets, normalization methods, or weights may produce different ORDER values for the same Flow.

---

## 8. Progressive Analysis

NFF can analyze Containers individually or progressively accumulate them.

For example:

$$
C_1
\rightarrow
C_1+C_2
\rightarrow
C_1+C_2+C_3
\rightarrow
\ldots
\rightarrow
C_1+C_2+\ldots+C_n
$$

Progressive analysis allows NFF to observe how detected structure evolves as additional information becomes available.

This is particularly relevant when analyzing a Flow in real time.

---

## 9. Flow Comparison

NFF Signatures can be compared to determine whether different Flows exhibit similar structural characteristics.

A simple Euclidean distance between two Signatures can be expressed as:

$$
d(S_a, S_b)
=
\sqrt{
\sum_i
(S_{a,i} - S_{b,i})^2
}
$$

A smaller distance indicates greater similarity according to the selected NUA measurements.

Other distance or similarity metrics can also be used depending on the structure of the Signature.

---

## 10. Real-Time Analysis

NFF can operate continuously as new data arrives.

New Containers are created and analyzed over time, producing an evolving ORDER sequence:

$$
ORDER(t_1),\ ORDER(t_2),\ ORDER(t_3),\ \ldots
$$

The resulting ORDER sequence can itself be treated as another Flow.

This creates a recursive relationship between raw observations and higher-level structural representations.

---

## 11. Recursive Analysis

An advanced extension of NFF is to analyze the ORDER sequence using NFF again.

$$
Flow
\rightarrow
NFF
\rightarrow
ORDER(t)
\rightarrow
NFF
\rightarrow
META\text{-}ORDER
$$

This enables structural analysis at multiple temporal scales.

The same principle can theoretically be extended to additional levels:

$$
ORDER
\rightarrow
META\text{-}ORDER
\rightarrow
META^2\text{-}ORDER
\rightarrow
\ldots
$$

Each level analyzes the structural behavior produced by the previous level.

## 12. Examples

### 12.1 Sine Wave

A sinusoidal Flow contains strong periodic structure:

$$
x(t)=\sin(t)
$$

Depending on the selected NUA units, NFF may detect characteristics such as:

* Strong periodicity
* Spectral concentration
* Predictability
* Stable temporal behavior

The resulting Signature reflects these characteristics.

---

### 12.2 Logistic Map

The Logistic Map is defined by:

$$
x_{n+1}=r x_n(1-x_n)
$$

For specific values of $r$, the system can exhibit chaotic behavior.

NFF can produce a Signature substantially different from that of a periodic Flow, depending on the selected NUA measurements.

This provides a useful mathematical test case for evaluating whether an NFF configuration can distinguish different classes of dynamical behavior.

## 14. Limitations

NFF does not currently provide a universal definition of order, chaos, or complexity.

Its results depend on several parameters, including:

* Selected NUA algorithms
* Normalization method
* Container Delay
* Sampling frequency
* Weighting strategy
* Available data
* Numerical precision

Therefore, an ORDER value only has meaning within a defined NFF configuration.

A rigorous evaluation requires controlled experiments involving known mathematical signals, noise, chaotic systems, different sampling rates, Container Delays, and perturbation levels.

NFF should consequently be considered a **framework for structural representation and comparison**, rather than a universal detector of mathematical order.

---

## 15. Fundamental Definition

<blockquote>
<strong>Normalized Factor Flow</strong> is a framework for transforming continuous data flows into normalized structural representations by segmenting the flow into temporal Containers, analyzing those Containers through multiple Normalized Units Amounts, and aggregating their normalized measurements into an NFF Signature and an ORDER value.
</blockquote>

The central objective of NFF is to extract measurable structure from continuous data flows and provide a numerical representation that can be compared across different flows and domains.

---

## 16. Conceptual Pipeline

The complete NFF process can be summarized as:

<p align="center">
  <strong>Flow → Containers → NUA Analysis → Normalization → NFF Signature → ORDER</strong>
</p>

For real-time systems:

<p align="center">
  <strong>Continuous Flow → Containers → NUA → Signature → ORDER(t)</strong>
</p>

For recursive analysis:

<p align="center">
  <strong>Flow → NFF → ORDER(t) → NFF → META-ORDER</strong>
</p>

---

## 17. Summary

NFF provides a structured methodology for transforming raw temporal data into a normalized representation of observable structure.

Its architecture is based on five fundamental elements:

1. **Flow** — the original sequence of observations.
2. **Containers** — temporal segmentation of the Flow.
3. **NUA** — independent measurements of structural properties.
4. **NFF Signature** — the normalized multidimensional representation.
5. **ORDER** — an aggregated scalar representation of the Signature.

The framework is intentionally domain-independent. Its usefulness depends on the mathematical properties measured by the NUA layer and on the configuration used to normalize and aggregate those measurements.
